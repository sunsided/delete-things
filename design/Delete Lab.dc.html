Lab / Interaction study 017

Forty ways to delete a file.

A rebuttal, in forty parts. Every button below is real: click it, hold it, swipe it, or regret it. Five of them are actually good UX. The other thirty-five are just for fun.

01The original
Into the bin
The one that started the beef, now without emoji or hard-coded pixel offsets.
02Actually good UX
Hold to confirm
Press and hold. Let go early and it backs off. Stops accidental taps without a modal.
03Mechanical
Shredder
The button turns into a slot. The document goes in whole and comes out in strips.
04Cosmic
Turn to dust
A snap, and it breaks into about two hundred grains that blow away left to right.
05Physics
Crumple & toss
Balled up and lobbed into the bin. Nothing but net.
06Actually good UX
Undo window
Deletes right away, then gives you four seconds to change your mind. Try clicking Undo.
07Cosmic
Event horizon
A singularity opens and pulls the label in, one letter at a time.
08Physics
Clean cut
One quick slash. Both halves slide apart along the cut and fall.
09Meta
Backspace
The word "Delete" deletes itself one letter at a time.
10Bureaucratic
Rubber stamp
Officially processed. Thud, a little shake, a little spatter.
11Physics
Gravity off… then on
Every letter falls to the floor and bounces on its own.
12Chaos
Short fuse
A lit fuse runs to the button. You know what happens next.
13Material
Eraser
Scrubbed out by hand, crumbs and all.
14Material
Meltdown
The button fills with ink, sags, drips and spreads into a puddle.
15Chaos
Glitch & CRT off
The colour channels split and the picture tears, then it switches off like an old TV.
16Cosmic
Paper plane
It folds itself into a plane, takes one wobbly loop and flies off.
17Material
Burn after reading
A glowing edge burns up from the bottom, with embers and smoke.
18Chaos
Overinflated
It puffs up, puffs up a bit more, trembles, and pops.
19Physics
Trapdoor
The floor opens and it drops through. The doors swing shut behind it.
20Chaos
Launch into the sun
A countdown, a rumble, a burst of smoke, and off it goes.
Volume II

Twenty more, because twenty wasn’t enough.

21Actually good UX
Slide to delete
Drag the handle all the way across. If you let go early it springs back.
22Actually good UX
Are you sure?
The button splits into Cancel and Yes, delete. A confirmation that stays in place instead of opening a modal.
23Mechanical
Robot vacuum
A little cleaning robot drives straight across and hoovers the whole button up.
24Material
Sands of time
The button turns to sand and drains through its own bottom edge into a pile.
25Mechanical
Tile flip
Like a train-station departure board: the tiles flip away in a diagonal wave.
26Material
Freeze & shatter
Frost creeps in, the button cracks into shards, and the shards fall.
27Material
Peel off
Lifted at one corner like a sticker and peeled away.
28Material
Washed away
It starts raining. The ink runs and slides off the page.
29Cosmic
Teleport
A beam locks on, squeezes it into a thin line, and sends it straight up.
30Theatrical
Magic trick
The curtains close and open again. The button is gone.
31Cosmic
Ghosted
It turns into a little ghost and drifts off without a word.
32Physics
Domino
Each letter tips over and knocks down the next one.
33Meta
The other side
It flips over. The back already says it’s done.
34Physics
Quicksand
Sand appears underneath and it slowly sinks out of sight, wobbling.
35Chaos
Struck by lightning
A flash and a bolt leave it charred, and it crumbles into ash.
36Actually good UX
Type to confirm
The button only works after you type the word. Good for deletes you can’t take back.
37Physics
One-tonne weight
Dropped from above and flattened. Dust puffs out on both sides.
38Meta
Full stop
It shrinks to a dot, which becomes the full stop at the end of “Deleted”.
39Bureaucratic
Redacted
Blacked out with a marker, then filed away as a redaction bar.
40Mechanical
Reeled in
A hook comes down, catches the button, and yanks it out of the frame.
41Bonus · nuclear
Delete the page
Why stop at one file? Everything on the page falls off the screen. Then everything comes back.
Inspired by a silly little delete button. It was right.
